The physics

Heat from acceleration

An observer with constant proper acceleration a traces out a hyperbola in spacetime. Light sent from behind the 45° line through the origin can never catch up with them: that line is their horizon, a distance c²/a behind them.

In quantum field theory, the state that an inertial observer calls the vacuum looks, to the accelerating observer, like a thermal bath at temperature

T = ħa / 2πckB

This is the Unruh effect. At everyday accelerations the temperature is far too small to measure: standing on Earth, it is about 4 × 10⁻²⁰ K.

It is the same physics as Hawking radiation. Replace a by the surface gravity κ of a black hole's horizon and you get the Hawking temperature, T = ħκ / 2πckB. Both come from a horizon and from the way the vacuum is entangled across it.
