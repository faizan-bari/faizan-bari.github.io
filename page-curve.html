The physics

About the model

The black hole is an ordinary four-dimensional Schwarzschild black hole that loses mass to Hawking radiation. Its mass falls as M(t) = M₀(1 − t/tev)1/3, and its Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, S = 4πGM²kB/ħc, falls with the square of the mass.

I assume the radiation's coarse-grained entropy grows by exactly as much as the black hole's entropy falls. For real emission into massless particles it grows somewhat faster, about one and a half times as fast, which moves the Page time earlier. In this simple model the Page time comes when the black hole has lost half its entropy: at about 65% of its lifetime, with 71% of its mass still left.

The total state of black hole and radiation stays pure if evaporation is unitary, so the radiation's entropy can never exceed the black hole's Bekenstein–Hawking entropy. That is why the true curve has to turn over. Showing how it turns over, from the gravitational path integral, was the breakthrough of 2019: a new saddle with an "island" behind the horizon takes over at the Page time.

In my project I study the same question in JT gravity, a two-dimensional model in which the black hole is coupled to a bath that collects its radiation.
