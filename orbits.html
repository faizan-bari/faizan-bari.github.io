The physics

Why orbits misbehave

For a particle moving in the plane around a Schwarzschild black hole, energy E and angular momentum L per unit mass are conserved, and the radial motion obeys (dr/dτ)² = E² − V(r) with

V(r) = (1 − 2M/r)(1 + L²/r²) = 1 − 2M/r + L²/r² − 2ML²/r³

The first three terms are Newton's gravity plus the centrifugal barrier. The last one is general relativity. It pulls harder close in, which makes orbits precess, puts the last stable circular orbit at r = 6M, and lets a particle with too little angular momentum fall straight in. Mercury's orbit precesses by 43 arcseconds per century for the same reason; near a black hole the shift can be hundreds of degrees per orbit.

The orbit is computed by integrating d²u/dφ² + u = M/L² + 3Mu², with u = 1/r. The particle moves at a pace set by its own proper time.
