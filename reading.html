Reading

What I'm reading

A few papers I keep coming back to, with a line on each.

Where it starts

Black holes and thermodynamics

  • 1975 · Commun. Math. Phys. 43, 199

    Particle creation by black holes

    S. W. Hawking

    Where it all starts: a quantum field on the background of a collapsing star ends up in a thermal state at late times.

  • 2001 · arXiv:gr-qc/9912119

    The thermodynamics of black holes

    R. M. Wald

    A careful review of black-hole thermodynamics, from the four laws to the generalised second law.

Two dimensions

JT gravity and the Page curve

  • 2022 · arXiv:2210.10846

    Solvable models of quantum black holes: a review on Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity

    T. G. Mertens, G. J. Turiaci

    Where JT gravity comes from, its classical solutions, the Schwarzian, and how to quantise it exactly.

  • 2018 · arXiv:1804.01081

    The factorization problem in Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity

    D. Harlow, D. Jafferis

    Quantises pure JT gravity by hand and finds that its Hilbert space does not split into a left and a right part.

  • 2019 · arXiv:1908.10996

    The Page curve of Hawking radiation from semiclassical geometry

    A. Almheiri, R. Mahajan, J. Maldacena, Y. Zhao

    Computes the Page curve of a JT black hole evaporating into a bath, using quantum extremal surfaces.

  • 2020 · arXiv:2006.06872

    The entropy of Hawking radiation

    A. Almheiri, T. Hartman, J. Maldacena, E. Shaghoulian, A. Tajdini

    The standard review of how the semiclassical gravitational path integral, through islands, gives the Page curve.

Observables

Algebras in gravity

  • 2023 · arXiv:2301.07257

    Algebras and states in JT gravity

    G. Penington, E. Witten

    Builds the algebra of observables of JT gravity with matter and shows that its entropy is the generalised entropy. The paper my current project starts from.

  • 2021 · arXiv:2112.12828

    Gravity and the crossed product

    E. Witten

    How coupling to gravity turns the Type III algebra of a quantum field into a Type II algebra, which has a trace and so a well-defined entropy.

  • 2023 · arXiv:2302.01958

    Notes on the type classification of von Neumann algebras

    J. Sorce

    A physicist's guide to what Type I, II and III von Neumann algebras are, and why the differences matter.
