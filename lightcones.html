Light cones near a black hole

Light from any event can only travel along the edges of its light cone. Move closer to a black hole and watch the cones tip over, until at the horizon even outgoing light can't get away.

The physics

What you're seeing

The diagram shows radius r across and time up. Each wedge is the future light cone of one event: light leaving that event moves along the wedge's two edges, and anything with mass stays inside it.

The first view uses ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, built so that infalling light always travels at 45°. Then the outgoing edge of a cone satisfies dr/dt̃ = (r − 2M)/(r + 2M). Far away that is close to 1, an ordinary upright cone. At the horizon, r = 2M, it is 0: light aimed straight out stays where it is. Inside, it is negative, so every edge of the cone points to smaller r, and every future ends at the singularity at r = 0.

In Schwarzschild coordinates the cones seem to pinch shut as they approach the horizon and turn on their side inside it. That is a fault of the coordinates, which break down at r = 2M, not of spacetime: someone falling in crosses the horizon in a finite time on their own clock.
